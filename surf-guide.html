Hiriketiya Surf Spot Guide

Understanding the waves of Hiriketiya Bay. Two distinct surf zones tailored for different skill sets.

🏖️ The Beach Sandbar

Swell Direction: South, Southwest
Bottom Type: Soft Sand
Best Tide: All Tides (Mid incoming preferred)
Difficulty: Beginner & Kids

Located inside the safe horseshoe bay. Offers gentle, rolling white-water foam waves and slow-peeling green waves breaking over shallow sand. Perfect for standing up on a softboard for the first time without any reef hazards.

🌊 The Point Reef Left

Swell Direction: South, Southwest (Needs 3ft+)
Bottom Type: Flat Coral Reef Shelf
Best Tide: Mid to High Tide (Dangerous at low)
Difficulty: Intermediate & Advanced

A fast, peeling left-hand point reef break located on the western corner of the bay. Can generate long, peeling left-hand rides with hollow sections when a good south swell rolls in. Requires careful lineup navigation and reef boots.

Beginner Surf Guides & Techniques

Essential techniques and local spot rules to help you progress safely in the water (Auto-swapping cards).

🚣‍♂️
Fundamental

1. Paddling Technique

Keep your chest high, arch your back slightly, and look forward. Paddle with deep, alternating arm strokes close to the rails of your board to gain maximum momentum before catching a wave.

💡 Pro Tip: Cup your hands slightly for maximum water displacement.
⚡
Pop-Up

2. Pop-Up Mechanics

In one explosive motion, push your chest up and bring your feet underneath you. Land in a low, wide stance with knees bent for balance, feet centered along the stringer.

💡 Pro Tip: Look at the open wave face, never look down at your feet.
⚖️
Priority Rules

3. Wave Right-Of-Way

The surfer closest to the peak (where the wave breaks first) has right-of-way. Always look both ways before paddling into a wave to ensure you aren't dropping in on someone else.

💡 Pro Tip: Call out your line ("Going Left!" / "Going Right!") early.
📊
Ocean IQ

4. Reading Swells

Look at the horizon for approaching lines. Green, unbroken wave faces are ideal for surfing. Observe the angle of the swell to determine if a wave will peel left or right.

💡 Pro Tip: Angle your board slightly toward the shoulder before pop-up.
🐢
Board Control

5. Turtle Roll Technique

When paddling out on a foamie/longboard into broken whitewater, flip upside down under the wave while clamping rails firmly to your chest, letting the wave roll over you.

💡 Pro Tip: Pull nose downward underwater so whitewater won't rip it away.
🧘
Balance

6. Stance & Balance

Maintain an athletic surfer stance: feet shoulder-width apart, knees flexible and bent, weight 60% front foot for speed, and 40% back foot over the fins for steering.

💡 Pro Tip: Point your front foot 45° forward for easier hip rotation.
🌊
Wave Riding

7. Generating Speed

Compress in the wave trough and extend up onto the open wave face. Pumping up and down the wave face generates momentum to pass fast sections and stay in the pocket.

💡 Pro Tip: Your board always follows your eyes and leading arm.
🛟
Safety

8. Rip Current Safety

If caught in an offshore channel current, stay calm on your surfboard. Never paddle directly against the flow — paddle parallel to the shoreline to exit the current corridor.

💡 Pro Tip: Never let go of your surfboard — it is your lifeline.

Surf Safety & Lineup Etiquette

Surfing is all about sharing the ocean. Respect the local lineup rules to ensure a safe and fun session.

👑

Right of Way

The surfer closest to the breaking peak has priority. Do not drop in on another surfer's line.

🚫

No Snaking

Do not paddle around other surfers to steal peak priority. Wait your turn in the lineup cycle.

🤝

Paddling Wide

When paddling back out to the lineup, paddle wide through the channel, not through the active wave zone.

🪂

Control Your Board

Always hold on to your leash. Never let go of your surfboard, as it can collide with and injure others.
